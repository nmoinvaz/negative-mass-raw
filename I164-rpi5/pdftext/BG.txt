We give an independent closed-form construction of rotating ﬁve-dimensional Ellis–Bronnikov
wormholes with equal angular momenta and analyze the radial instability of the reﬂection-symmetric
family. The nontrivial coupled metric equations reduce exactly to an open A2 Toda system. The
general two-ended solution is expressed in terms of elementary Toda tau functions; the symmetric
subclass reduces to a particularly simple one-parameter family interpolating between the static
wormhole and the extremal equal-spin Myers–Perry geometry. On this exact background we revisit
the cohomogeneity-one perturbation problem. Besides the ordinary unstable mode of the static
wormhole, rotation renders the descendant of its non-normalizable zero mode normalizable. The
two purely imaginary unstable frequencies coalesce at |J|/A = 0.100628, where their eigenvectors
coalesce as well, identifying a second-order exceptional point. Beyond it the pair continues as
ω± = ±ωR + iωI with ωI > 0: the apparent loss of the radial instability found in the original
real-eigenvalue treatment is therefore not stabilization, but a transition to an oscillatory instability.
We follow this branch deep into the rapidly rotating regime, where its growth rate becomes strongly
suppressed.

Introduction. The Ellis–Bronnikov wormhole [1, 2] is
the canonical asymptotically ﬂat traversable wormhole
supported by a phantom scalar ﬁeld. The static Ellis–Bronnikov wormhole is, however, known to be linearly
unstable against spherically symmetric perturbations: in
four dimensions it possesses a single exponentially growing radial mode, as established by several complementary
formulations [3–6], while nonlinear evolutions lead either
to collapse or to an expanding wormhole conﬁguration
[7, 8]. The instability persists for the higher-dimensional
generalizations, which likewise possess a radial unstable
mode [9].
Rotating generalizations of the Ellis–Bronnikov wormhole were ﬁrst studied perturbatively in the slow-rotation
regime [10, 11], while fully nonlinear rotating solutions
were subsequently constructed numerically [12, 13]. The
latter form continuous families connecting the static
Ellis–Bronnikov wormhole to the extremal Kerr limit.
This raised the question whether rotation might also
weaken or remove the radial instability, which was addressed perturbatively for slowly rotating wormholes
[14, 15]. More recently, quasinormal modes of rapidly
rotating wormholes were calculated in nonradial sectors
[16], while fully nonlinear time evolutions have conﬁrmed
that rotation substantially slows the instability [17].
In ﬁve dimensions, imposing equal angular momenta
enhances the rotational symmetry suﬃciently to preserve
cohomogeneity one. The resulting rotating wormholes
were constructed numerically in Ref. [18], where their approach to the extremal equal-spin Myers–Perry black hole
[19] and their radial stability were also studied. More recent numerical work explored strongly asymmetric con-

ﬁgurations [20].
Here we report two results. First, the complete stationary equations admit an exact Toda reduction and closedform solution. Second, using this exact background we
resolve the fate of the two radial unstable modes of the
reﬂection-symmetric family. The merger previously interpreted as the disappearance of the instability is an
exceptional point (EP): when the spectral problem is continued into the complex plane, the two modes persist as
an oscillatory unstable pair.
Exceptional points have recently been identiﬁed in
black-hole quasinormal-mode spectra, including massive scalar perturbations of Kerr and scalar or
gravito-electromagnetic perturbations of hairy and
Kerr–Newman black holes. These examples concern
damped quasinormal modes [21–24]. The associated resonant excitation and non-Hermitian mode interaction near
EPs have also been investigated in the context of blackhole ringdown [25–28]. To the best of our knowledge, the
present case provides the ﬁrst explicit identiﬁcation, including eigenvector coalescence, of an exceptional point
formed by two dynamically unstable modes, and the ﬁrst
such identiﬁcation in the perturbation spectrum of a fully
self-gravitating horizonless compact object.
Closely analogous spectral behavior was already found
for supercritical charged Ellis–Bronnikov wormholes,
where two purely imaginary unstable radial modes merge
and subsequently acquire opposite real parts while retaining the same growth rate. Although this merger was
not interpreted in terms of exceptional-point topology in
Refs. [29, 30], it can now be recognized as the same characteristic spectral mechanism.

Discussion. The exact Toda solution describing a
fully rotating ﬁve-dimensional wormhole of equal angular momenta is an intriguing analytical solution. The
earlier stability analysis of Ref. [18], based on a numerically constructed background, suggested a restoration of

radial stability as rapid rotation sets in. In this work
we show that the two unstable modes coalesce at the EP
at |J|/A ≃ 0.100628 and proceed to exhibit an oscillatory instability. This realizes, in an unstable sector, the
same non-Hermitian mode-coalescence mechanism that
underlies the resonant behavior discussed for black-hole
quasinormal modes near exceptional points [25–28].
The endpoint is also structurally interesting. The
scalar charge vanishes and the background approaches
extremal Myers–Perry, while the unstable frequency is
driven towards zero. It will require a dedicated nearextremal analysis to examine whether the limiting mode
is a threshold mode at the wormhole-to-black-hole transition, or is a standard Myers–Perry quasinormal mode.
Nonetheless, our central conclusion remains intact that
the symmetric rotating ﬁve-dimensional wormholes are
linearly unstable in the cohomogeneity-one gravitational
sector.
Note added. After our exact construction and stability analysis had been obtained, the preprint by Nozawa
[31] appeared, giving an independent exact construction
of the rotating ﬁve-dimensional wormhole family from an
A1 ⊕ A2 Toda reduction and analyzing its global geometry. The present work is complementary in its emphasis
on the radial spectrum, the exceptional point, and the
oscillatory instability beyond the merger.
